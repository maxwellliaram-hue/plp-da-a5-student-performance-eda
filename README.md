# Student Performance EDA: County Education Office

## Project summary
Exploratory data analysis of 1,200 students (`student_performance.csv`, generated by `generate_data.py` with seed 55) to help a County Director of Education decide where to target a learner-support programme. `student_eda.ipynb` covers Structure, Univariate, Bivariate, Multivariate and Hypotheses, then Findings, Recommendations and Limitations. It creates `average_score` and `performance_band` (At Risk < 60, Meeting 60-74, Exceeding >= 75), a correlation matrix, pivot tables testing the school-type gap, and an `np.polyfit` trend of score on study hours.

## How to run
```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python generate_data.py              # creates student_performance.csv
jupyter notebook student_eda.ipynb   # Kernel > Restart & Run All
```
The five chart PNGs are stored in the repository root. Running the notebook again regenerates them there.

## Five key insights
1. Only 8.2% of learners (99 of 1,200) are At Risk; 56.0% are Meeting and 35.8% Exceeding.
2. Study hours is the strongest genuine factor (r = 0.70): about +2.1 points per extra weekly hour, R² about 0.50.
3. Attendance matters moderately (r = 0.36): 24% of learners attending under 70% are At Risk versus 2% at 85%+.
4. Risk is concentrated in Public schools (94 of 99 At Risk learners) and in the lowest study-hours quartile. Private schools score about 5.5 points higher, and the gap persists across every attendance band and study quartile.
5. County and gender do not separate learners: Nairobi has the highest At Risk share (9.2%), but county differences are consistent with chance (permutation p about 0.9).

## Recommendations
1. Target by learner profile, not county: Public-school learners with low study hours and attendance below 70%.
2. Pair attendance follow-up with structured study support, and track At Risk share to test whether it works.

## Limitations
Observational, cross-sectional data: associations are not causal, income, prior attainment and teacher quality are unmeasured, At Risk counts per county are small, scores are capped at 100, and there are no dates for trend analysis.
